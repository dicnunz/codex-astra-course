Course Home

This course studies how to use Codex with GPT-6 Astra as an engineering system: how to state work, supply evidence, choose tools and reasoning effort, preserve context, delegate safely, and decide whether the result is actually finished.

The course takes a systematic rather than promotional approach. The readings pair current OpenAI product documentation with first-hand writing from people building and using Codex, especially field reports about Astra, computer use, long-running goals, and durable context. The emphasis is on reconstructing practical claims, comparing competing workflow choices, and testing them against observable results.

Begin with the Syllabus. Then follow the Calendar in order. Each of the first 22 sessions has a compact lecture handout. External readings are linked rather than copied into this package.

Course structure

Two one-hour lectures and one one-hour recitation per week; 25 sessions; 22 lecture handouts; brief quizzes; one oral presentation; three short papers; one final paper; and a required rewrite of Paper 1.

Central questions

What should a task specification contain? When should Astra infer details rather than ask? When does more reasoning effort help? What belongs in the prompt, AGENTS.md, a skill, a plugin, or a tool? When should work be split across subagents or worktrees? What evidence should count as verification? When should the agent stop and hand the work back?
