Course Meeting Times

Lectures: 2 sessions / week, 1 hour / session

Prerequisites

There are no prerequisites for this course. You should be willing to inspect files, run or read tests, and reason carefully about what counts as evidence that a task is complete. Prior Git knowledge is useful but not assumed.

Course Description

This course has two goals. The first is to introduce you to the central problems that arise when using Codex with GPT-6 Astra: converting an intention into a task, deciding what context to provide, choosing a reasoning level and tool surface, keeping durable instructions useful rather than obstructive, coordinating parallel work, and verifying an agent's claims.

The second goal is to develop your ability to reason about these systems for yourself. You will learn to reconstruct workflow claims from documentation and practitioner reports, distinguish a plausible-looking artifact from verified work, identify hidden assumptions in task specifications, compare alternatives, and defend your choices with evidence from actual runs.

Requirements

Readings. Read assigned material carefully before class. Product documentation changes; use the linked current page and note material changes. Some practitioner pieces are intentionally concrete and experience-based. Treat their recommendations as claims to evaluate, not rules to repeat.

Lectures and quizzes. Attendance at lectures is expected. Many lectures include a brief quiz on distinctions introduced in the readings or the previous handout.

Recitations. Attend and participate each week. Recitation work centers on task diagnosis, prompt and instruction comparison, verification design, and review of small Codex runs.

Presentations. Each student gives one formal presentation on a workflow claim, case, or failure mode agreed with the recitation instructor. A good presentation states the claim precisely, identifies the evidence, gives at least one serious objection or counterexample, and says what would change the conclusion.

Papers. You will write three 1,000-to-1,200 word papers and one 2,000-to-2,400 word final paper, for a minimum of 5,000 words. You will revise and resubmit Paper 1 after meeting with your recitation instructor.

Attribution and Evidence

Your writing must distinguish your own analysis from claims taken from OpenAI documentation, repositories, employee writing, experiments, and classmates. Direct quotations require quotation marks and a citation. Paraphrases and borrowed ideas require a citation as well. Logs, screenshots, benchmark outputs, diffs, or test results should be identified closely enough that another reader could understand what they show and what they do not show.
