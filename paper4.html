Paper 4

2,000-2,400 words. Due in Session 25.

Choose one question. State the claim you are evaluating precisely. Reconstruct the strongest relevant reasoning from the assigned sources, defend your own position, address a serious objection, and use concrete Codex behavior or a proposed test where it bears on the argument.

  1. Design and defend an end-to-end operating doctrine for a demanding Codex+Astra engineering task. It must cover task specification, context, reasoning effort, tool choice, permissions, delegation, verification, review, and stopping criteria. Each rule needs a reason and at least one exception.
  2. Is Astra's stronger initiative best handled by more prescriptive prompts or by clearer boundaries plus better evidence? Develop both positions before defending one. Use official model guidance and at least one OpenAI employee case.
  3. Argue for a minimal customization stack for a real repository. Decide what belongs in AGENTS.md, skills, plugins or connectors, subagents, and automation. Your proposal must remove at least one tempting layer as unnecessary and explain why.
  4. Compare two strategies for improving a difficult task: increasing reasoning effort and improving the environment, references, tools, or verification loop. State conditions under which each dominates and propose a small evaluation that could distinguish them.
  5. What should count as finished autonomous work? Develop a general standard that distinguishes completion, verification, reviewability, and authorization. Apply it to a coding task, a browser task, and a cross-application task.

Citations

Cite documentation, employee writing, and any run evidence closely enough that another reader could locate it. Do not submit agent-generated prose as your own analysis.
